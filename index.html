01 / HOW IT FOUND ME

Some stories
stay with you.

An old friend I eventually lost contact with introduced me to JoJo's Bizarre Adventure. I still remember being pulled in by the first part: a Victorian-era clash of brotherhood, rivalry, and the struggle between good and evil. It was unlike anything I had watched before, and it immediately had my attention.

There is a bittersweet feeling attached to the series now. JoJo was once an interest I shared with someone else; these days, I carry that appreciation on my own. The connection has changed, but the stories have stayed with me. I still follow the weekly Steel Ball Run anime releases, and my bookshelf has become its own little tribute—I have collected almost every English edition of the manga.

What began as a recommendation turned into something I return to again and again. JoJo is comfort, surprise, spectacle, and a reminder that an old interest can become your own.

02 / THE JOJO FORMULA

It's not just a fight.
It's a mind game.

JoJo has plenty of wild physical showdowns, but the battles that really hook me are the ones built around a puzzle. Characters study each other's abilities, look for loopholes, bluff, improvise, and try to outthink an opponent who may have an utterly ridiculous power.

That is what makes a victory feel so good. It is not always about who can hit harder. Sometimes the winning move is the one nobody else saw coming.

04 / CASE STUDY: STARDUST CRUSADERS

A sick baby.
A plane crash.
A nightmare.

LIGHT SPOILERS FOR PART 3

Picture this: Jotaro and the Stardust Crusaders are on a long journey toward Egypt to face DIO. They take a plane to speed things up—and end up traveling with a sick baby named Mannish Boy. The baby is tied to a Stand called Death Thirteen, which attacks people in their dreams.

Kakyoin realizes that the fight is happening in a nightmare world and finds a way to turn the enemy's rules against it. The encounter even ends with the plane crashing. It is a perfect example of JoJo's imagination: a bizarre detour, a battle of wits, and a solution you probably would never have guessed.

06 / BEYOND THE PAGE

The world is part
of the adventure.

JoJo's stories feel so alive partly because they draw from more than fantasy. The series moves across countries, cultures, historical periods, art, and music. Hirohiko Araki has spoken about research trips to places featured in JoJo and how experiencing a location's culture and art can leave a lasting impression on his work.

That care gives the adventures a sense of place. The setting is never just a backdrop—it becomes part of the story's personality.

07 / WHY I KEEP COMING BACK

The next page
is always unexpected.

JoJo's Bizarre Adventure is one of those series I can revisit without losing interest. It can be hilarious, intense, heartfelt, grotesque, stylish, and strangely educational—sometimes all in the same episode. I love it because I never feel completely prepared for what comes next.
